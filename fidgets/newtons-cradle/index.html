How to play the Newton’s cradle

Grab a ball on either end, pull it out to the side, and let go. It swings down, clacks into the row, and the ball on the far end swings out. Grab a ball in the middle to pull several out at once. Press Stop to bring everything to rest.

How does a Newton’s cradle work?

A Newton’s cradle is a row of steel balls hanging side by side from a frame. When the first ball hits the row, its push travels through the balls in the middle and comes out the other end, so the last ball swings out at almost the same speed. Pull back two balls and two swing out on the other side.

It’s named after Isaac Newton, whose laws of motion explain how it works, and it has been a classic desk toy since the 1960s. Real cradles lose a little energy with every clack, and this one does too, so the swings get smaller until it settles.

Can I pull back more than one ball?

Yes. Grab a ball in the middle and pull it outward, and every ball on that side comes with it. Let go, and the same number swing out on the other end.

Why does the cradle slow down?

Every clack loses a little energy, just like on a real one, so the swings get smaller until it stops. Pull a ball back to start it again.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes. It works on iPhone, Android, tablets, and computers, and many phones give a small vibration with every clack.
