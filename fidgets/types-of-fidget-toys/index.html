Types of fidget toys

From spinners to Pop Its, here are the main kinds of fidget toys, what each one is like, and which ones you can try free in your browser.

Fidget toys are small, hand-sized things you can press, spin, squish, or click. There are hundreds of them, but most fit into a handful of families. Here’s a quick tour.

Pop and press

Pop Its (push poppers)

Silicone trays covered in bubbles that push in with a soft pop. When they’re all popped, you flip the tray over and pop them back from the other side.

Play Pop It online →

Dimple fidgets

A smaller cousin of the Pop It, usually just a few bubbles on a keychain, so it fits in a pocket.

Bubble wrap

The original popping toy. Each bubble only pops once, which is exactly why a sheet that never runs out is so handy.

Pop bubble wrap online →

Keycap fidgets and keyboards

A single mechanical keyboard key, or a small pad of them, made just for pressing. Fans pick switches by sound, from sharp clicky ones to deep, thocky ones.

Try the mechanical keyboard →

Click and flip

Clickers and tally counters

A metal hand counter with a button on top. Each press adds one to the count and makes a crisp click.

Use the online clicker →

Fidget cubes

A small cube with something different on every side, like buttons, a switch, a dial, a joystick, and a rolling ball.

Switch boards

Panels of toggle switches, buttons, and latches. Think of a light switch you can flip all day.

Flip the light switch →

Click pens

Pens with an extra-satisfying click, sometimes with spinners or sliders built into the barrel.

Spin and roll

Fidget spinners

A center bearing with two or three weighted arms. Give it a flick and it keeps spinning for a surprisingly long time.

Spin a fidget spinner online →

Spinner rings

A ring with an outer band that spins freely around the inner one, so you can fidget with a single finger.

Infinity cubes

Eight little cubes hinged together that fold over and over on themselves without ever reaching an end.

Flippy chains and magnetic sliders

Bike-chain links or magnetic pieces that you roll, slide, and snap together between your fingers.

Squish and stretch

Stress balls and squishies

Soft balls and slow-rising foam shapes that spring back after you squeeze them. Squishies often come shaped like animals or food.

Squeeze the virtual stress ball →

Slime and putty

Stretchy, moldable goo you can pull, poke, and fold. Putty is firmer and holds its shape, while slime is gooier.

Stretchy strings and pop tubes

Rubbery strings you can pull and wrap around your fingers, and ribbed plastic tubes that pop and click as you stretch and bend them.

Swing and watch

Newton’s cradles

A row of hanging steel balls. Pull one back, let go, and the clack passes through the row to the ball on the far end.

Play the Newton’s cradle →

Liquid motion timers

Sealed tubes of colored drops that drip slowly from one chamber to the next. Flip it over and watch it go again.

Ball pits and marble toys

Anything with lots of little balls to pour, roll, and watch tumble into place.

How to pick a fidget toy

A few quick questions make it easy:

  • Does it need to be quiet? Spinner rings, putty, stretchy strings, and squishies make almost no noise. Clickers, pop tubes, and clicky keys do.
  • Will it fit in a pocket? Dimple keychains, spinner rings, and infinity cubes go anywhere. Newton’s cradles and switch boards live on a desk.
  • Do you like to press, spin, or squish? Pick the family that matches what your hands already like to do.
  • Will it hold up? Metal and silicone toys last a long time. Slime dries out, and bubble wrap runs out.

What are the most popular types of fidget toys?

Some of the best known are fidget spinners, Pop Its, fidget cubes, stress balls, infinity cubes, and slime. Most fidget toys fall into a few families: things you pop, click, spin, squish, or watch.

What are quiet fidget toys?

Spinner rings, putty, stretchy strings, squishies, and stress balls make almost no noise, so they’re easy to use in class, at work, or on a call.
